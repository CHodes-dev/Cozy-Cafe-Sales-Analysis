# Business Understanding

Explaining how businesses operate and make profits, particularly in the Makassar region.

## 1. Cafe Business Model

### 1.1 Value Proposition <br>
A cafe offers more than food and beverages; it also provides an attractive environment and a comfortable space for socializing, studying, and working. Visually appealing interiors can attract visitors, particularly Gen Z customers who share their experiences on social media, while functional facilities encourage students and workers to stay longer and return.

### 1.2 Revenue Streams <br>
A cafe generates its primary revenue from food and beverage sales, including coffee, non-coffee drinks, and meals or snacks that can increase the average purchase value. Additional revenue can come from room rentals, merchandise, coffee beans, and event catering, while digital delivery platforms and partnerships through events such as art exhibitions and live music provide further opportunities to reach customers and diversify income.

### 1.3 Sales Channels <br>
Cafes use on-premise and off-premise sales channels to accommodate different customer needs. On-premise channels include dine-in services, room rentals, and in-store retail, which utilize the cafe's physical space, while off-premise channels include third-party delivery platforms, direct orders, pickup services, and business-to-business supplies for external events, allowing cafes to generate revenue beyond their seating capacity.

### 1.4 Business Models <br>
Cafe business models vary according to their operational focus and competitive strategy. Grab-and-go cafes prioritize speed, affordability, and high transaction volume; experiential cafes emphasize atmosphere, comfort, and customer experience; and specialty coffee shops focus on coffee quality and barista expertise. Cafes can also combine volume-based sales, premium pricing, and customer loyalty strategies through menu cross-subsidization, time-based space optimization, and digital loyalty programs to diversify revenue and mitigate business risk.

## 2. Customer Behavior

### 2.1 Customer Segments <br>
Cafe customers can be grouped according to their motivations, including socializers, remote workers and students, content creators, and coffee enthusiasts. In Makassar, customer segments may also reflect local social and cultural habits, including Gen Z and creative communities seeking aesthetic spaces, students and remote workers looking for comfortable workspaces, business professionals needing meeting facilities, and families seeking comfortable dining environments. The document also identifies Tamalanrea and BTP as student-oriented areas, Panakkukang and Hertasning as business and family-oriented areas, and Rappocini and Jalan Tupai as areas associated with creative communities and aesthetic cafes.

### 2.2 Purchase Behavior <br>
Customer purchasing behavior can be examined through purchase frequency, product preferences, items per transaction, and average spending. The document describes differences between regular office workers and recreational visitors, identifies iced palm sugar milk coffee, traditional coffee, matcha, snacks, and rice- or noodle-based meals as potential customer favorites in Makassar, and notes that group visits may generate additional shared-food purchases. It also provides estimated spending ranges of IDR 15,000–25,000 per person at small coffee kiosks, IDR 45,000–75,000 at aesthetic or work-friendly cafes, and IDR 90,000–150,000 or more at premium specialty cafes and lounges.

### 2.3 Customer Retention <br>
Customer retention depends on product quality, service, atmosphere, pricing, and location. Consistent taste, menu variety, friendly and efficient service, comfortable facilities, and an appropriate balance between price and perceived value can encourage repeat visits. In Makassar, social interactions, long cafe visits, and convenient parking are also highlighted as relevant considerations, suggesting that cafes need to provide both a satisfying product and an environment that meets customers' functional and social needs.

## 3. Business Context

### 3.1 Economic Conditions and Consumer Purchasing Power <br>
Makassar's economic conditions, consumer purchasing power, and student population provide important context for understanding cafe demand. The document highlights economic growth, inflation, household consumption, and the presence of universities as factors potentially influencing food and beverage spending. These indicators can help explain the broader market environment, although their actual effects on cafe sales need to be assessed using reliable, up-to-date data.

### 3.2 Cafe Market Competition in Makassar <br>
The cafe market in Makassar is described as highly competitive, with businesses differentiating themselves through atmosphere, location visibility, lifestyle appeal, facilities, and customer experience rather than competing solely on price. The document reports 807 cafes in 2023 and identifies Tamalanrea and BTP, Panakkukang and Hertasning, and Rappocini and Jalan Tupai as key cafe areas. It also describes different menu price segments, hybrid cafe concepts, extended operating hours, and non-product differentiation strategies, such as comfortable spaces, distinctive interior designs, and modern presentations of local food.

## 4. Revenue Drivers

Cafe sales performance is influenced by transaction volume, average order value, product mix, and cost structure. Transaction volume measures the number of orders, while average order value (AOV) represents the average amount spent per order and can be calculated by dividing total sales by total orders. Product mix analysis examines sales contributions across product categories, while cost structure includes cost of goods sold (COGS), labor, rent, and utilities. Together, these factors help explain sales performance and provide a foundation for evaluating profitability, provided that sufficient cost data is available.
